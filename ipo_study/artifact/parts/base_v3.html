What the first days, weeks and months of 43 big IPOs say about ANT

The frenzy is real but short. The reversion is slow, uneven, and depends on the sector, the quarter and the rate regime the company lists into. This page buckets the cohort by market cap, sector, listing season and macro regime, then reads Anthropic against each cut.

Conclusions

Expect a squeeze in days 1 to 5 and fade risk from day 5 to day 63. The rate backdrop is the thing most likely to break the squeeze, and chasing the open has historically been the worst entry.

  • Timing. From the day-1 close the median IPO is +4% at day 5, −1.5% at day 21, −7% at day 63 and −23% at day 126. 58% trade below their offer price at some point in the first 63 days.
  • Intraday. The open is usually the high-water mark. The median opening print is +23% over the offer and the median close +19%; 60% of debuts close below their open, and the typical close sits 7.5% under the day's high. Buying the open lost a median 11% by day 63. IPOs in the hourly sample open late (median first print about 12:30 ET), and the debut-day high came in the first trading hour for 10 of 15 hourly-data IPOs.
  • Market cap. Every mega-cap in the sample reverted within 21 days (SpaceX −15%, Facebook −18%, Alibaba −6%; n = 3). Mid caps ($10B to $30B) were worst, −47% by month twelve.
  • Sector. Consumer internet fades first (−25% by day 63), fintech and crypto fade hardest (−41% by day 126), software, AI and semis hold up best (−3% by day 63). Anthropic sits in the last group.
  • Season. Q4 and Q1 listings pop most (+39%, +34%); Q3 listings fade worst (−33% by month six). November and December are top-three S&P months, and the S&P rose in 18 of 19 post-midterm three-month windows from 15 November.
  • Macro regime. After a 3-month rise in the 10-year yield above 30bp the median IPO was −18% at day 63, against +2% with flat yields. Anthropic lists with the 10-year at 5.15% (+46bp), a level the cohort has never seen.
  • Liquidity. Bitcoin, MSTR, gold and Nvidia were sold before both SpaceX and Anthropic while Fed net liquidity did not fall. That looks like private de-grossing to fund about $160B to $177B of mega-IPO supply in five months, though yields and the dollar explain the same moves.
  • Anthropic. Base case (about 50%) is SpaceX-like: pop of +15% to +35%, peak in days 2 to 5, back near the offer by day 63. Bull about 20%, bear about 30%. Watch the price against the $70 to $78 range, the 10-year, Nvidia, and Bitcoin after pricing.

The pop is quick, the fade takes months

Measured from the day-1 close, the price a frenzy buyer pays, the typical IPO is roughly flat after a week, a touch lower after a month, and about a quarter lower by month six. Only SpaceX has a post-COVID mega-cap path, and it followed the script: peak on day 3 (+31% from its first close), back below the $135 offer on day 23, trough on day 37.

Intraday: the open is usually the best price a seller gets

Daily open, high and low for all 43 debuts show a consistent shape: the median stock opens +23% over its offer, closes +19%, and ends the day 7.5% below its high; 60% close under their open. How big the opening pop is says nothing about the rest of the day (rank correlation +0.07). Hourly bars for the 15 IPOs since 2025 show the mechanics: the first print comes late (median about 12:30 ET), trading volume is heaviest in the first hour after it, and the high of the day arrives in that first trading hour for 10 of the 15. Day 2 starts weak: its first hourly bar is down in 80% of cases.

Sectors: consumer internet fades first, fintech fades hardest, software holds up best

Consumer internet (Airbnb, DoorDash, Reddit, Instacart) popped most (+45%) and was under water within a week. Fintech and crypto kept rallying for a month (+21%), then gave back a quarter and, by month six, 41%. Software, AI and semis, where Anthropic sits, popped +28% and lost only 3% by month three. Healthcare looks best but is four deals.

Seasonality: Q4 pops are biggest, Q3 listings fade worst, and the post-midterm tape helps

Q4 and Q1 listings had the largest day-1 pops (+39% and +34%) and Q2 the smallest (+14%). Q3 listings fared worst by month six (−33%). Each quarter has only 6 to 15 names and the sample is clustered in 2021, so read this as a tilt. The market itself leans the same way: November and December are two of the three best S&P months since 1950, and after a midterm election the S&P rose in 18 of 19 three-month windows from 15 November.

Macro regime: rising long yields are the worst backdrop, and ANT lists into one

When the 10-year yield had risen more than 30bp in the three months before listing, the median IPO was down 18% by day 63; with flat yields it was up 2%. High volatility (VIX above 22) bought the biggest pops (+56%) and the sharpest three-month give-back (−23%). Almost every IPO in the cohort listed with the S&P above its 200-day average, so the window itself tells you the market is healthy.

Liquidity: the same assets get sold before every mega-IPO

In the 14 trading days before SpaceX listed and in the run-up to Anthropic, Bitcoin, MSTR, gold and the AI complex fell while the S&P held up. Fed net liquidity did not fall (it rose about $43B into Anthropic as the Treasury cash balance was drawn down) and VIX stayed near 19. That reads as private de-grossing to fund the allocation, not a reserves squeeze. Yields at 5.15% and a firmer dollar explain the same moves, and two events cannot separate the two.

Q3 2026: 30% of deals priced below the range; the ten largest averaged −6% from offer; Oura and Holtec pulled their deals.

Supply is the other half. SpaceX ($75B to $86B), SK hynix ($26.5B) and Anthropic (about $58B to $64B) add up to roughly $160B to $177B in five months, against $104.8B of US IPO proceeds in all of Q2. After SpaceX listed, Bitcoin rose 23% and gold 6% over the next 63 trading days, so the pressure lifted once the supply cleared (one observation).

Reading Anthropic against every cut

Tailwinds

  • Q4 listing: biggest day-1 pops, and only 33% of Q4 names traded below offer in 63 days (6 deals).
  • Post-midterm tape: S&P up in 18 of 19 post-midterm windows from 15 Nov, average +7.7% over three months.
  • Software and AI: the most resilient sector, median −3% at day 63.
  • Float about 4.5%: tiny float and a fast-money crowd support a day 1 to 5 squeeze, as with SpaceX.
  • Valuation: roughly 16x to 17x run-rate revenue against about 39x forward sales for SpaceX.

Headwinds

  • Rising yields: 10Y at 5.15%, +46bp in three months. The cohort's rising-yield IPOs were −18% at day 63.
  • Mega size: every mega-cap in the sample reverted within 21 days (SpaceX −15%, Facebook −18%, Alibaba −6%); n = 3.
  • Cold tape: three straight quarters of negative IPO aftermarket returns; 30% of Q3 deals priced below range.
  • Risk-off in the neighbours: Nvidia −25%, Bitcoin −15%, MSTR −20% since the S-1 went public.
  • Unlocks: up to 10% early, then 180 days or Q1-27 results; more than 20 windows over a year.
Base · SpaceX-lite
~50%

Day-1 pop of +15% to +35%, peak on days 2 to 5 at +30% to +55% over the offer, down 10% to 20% from the day-1 close by day 21, near the offer by day 63.

Bull · scarcity holds
~20%

Prices at or above range, pop above 40%, and the low float plus index demand keep it well above the offer through month three. Needs yields to stop rising.

Bear · rates and AI-spend
~30%

Pop under 10%, below the offer within about three weeks, −20% to −30% by day 63. Raised from 25% in the first note because of the rising-yield result.

Odds are my judgment, loosely calibrated on the analogs above. They are not a model output or advice.

What to watch

  • The price against the $70 to $78 range, and day-1 volume. Above range with heavy volume is the squeeze setup.
  • The 10-year yield and Nvidia over the first two weeks. Both falling together was the bear trigger in 2021 and 2025.
  • Bitcoin, MSTR and gold after pricing. A bounce would be the "supply cleared" signal seen after SpaceX.
  • The opening print itself. Debuts in the sample opened around midday ET; the day-1 high has usually come in the first hour, and buying the open has lost a median 11% by day 63.
  • Any Nasdaq-100 inclusion date (SpaceX fell about 13% after being added) and the first sizeable unlock windows.

Data and method

Table view: all 43 IPOs

Universe. 43 notable US IPOs from Sep 2020 to Jun 2026 whose offer price I could pin down. It is a hand-picked set, not the full universe, so it leans toward large and well-known deals (selection bias) and has no 2022 names because the window was shut. Direct listings (Coinbase, Roblox, Palantir) and the SK hynix ADR are excluded.

Returns. Day 1 is the first close. Pop = day-1 close against the offer price. Later returns are measured from the day-1 close at trading days 5, 21, 63, 126 and 252. Prices from Yahoo Finance, unadjusted closes.

Market caps at offer are approximate (±20%) and sector labels are my own six-way grouping. Regimes use ^TNX, ^VIX, the S&P 500 and FRED effective fed funds the day before listing.

Small samples. Sector and quarter cells hold 4 to 15 names, regimes 6 to 30, and mega-caps 1 to 3. Medians are shown with n; none of this is statistically tested.

Anthropic facts (range $70 to $78, 825M shares, pricing 18 Nov, lock-up terms, run-rate revenue) come from secondary summaries of the S-1/A and press reports; verify against the filing. SpaceX offer price is $135 (Reuters, Investopedia). Cross-asset moves are my calculation from Yahoo and FRED. Q3 IPO class statistics: Renaissance Capital 3Q 2026 review. Not investment advice.
