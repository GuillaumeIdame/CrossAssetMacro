What 43 IPOs and 11 large listings say about trading Anthropic

Close-to-close and intraday evidence on the IPO stock itself and on the assets around it, with confidence intervals, significance tests and implied Sharpe ratios. Most patterns people quote about IPOs are real on average and weak statistically; this page separates the two.

Conclusions

One relationship survives a multiple-testing correction: demand predicts the pop. Getting an allocation also pays, reliably. Everything else on this page (sector, season, rates, float, cross-asset liquidity) is a tilt with confidence intervals that include zero. For Anthropic that means a conditional plan, sized for low confidence.

    Float and demand: the hot-deal effect is the one that holds up

    Oversubscription multiples are only published for a couple of deals (Reddit 5x, Gemini 20x), so this uses the standard proxy: where the final price landed against the first filed range, from the 424B4 prospectuses. Deals priced above their initial range popped +31% against +4% for the rest, and the size of the price revision predicts the pop strongly (rank correlation 0.67, q < 0.001). The same hot deals then gave back the most: −20% by day 63 (95% CI −28% to −2%) against +14%. The share of the company floated shows no reliable relationship with anything (all p > 0.2).

    Deal-structure buckets

    Intraday: the open is usually the best price a seller gets

    Daily open, high and low for all 43 debuts: the median stock opens +23% over its offer, closes +19%, and ends the day 7.5% below its high; 60% close under their open. Buying the open and holding to the close lost 1.1% on average (95% CI −4.3% to +2.2%, so not significant on its own). Hourly bars for the 14 debuts since 2025 with a complete first session show the mechanics: the first print comes late (median about 12:30 ET), volume is heaviest in the hour after it, and the high of the day came in that first hour for 10 of the 14. Day 2's first hourly bar was down for 79% of them.

    Sectors: big differences in the medians, none statistically

    Consumer internet faded first (−25% by day 63), fintech and crypto faded hardest (−41% by day 126), and software, AI and semis held up best (−3% by day 63). With 4 to 12 names per sector the confidence intervals overlap heavily, and a Kruskal-Wallis test cannot tell the sectors apart (q ≥ 0.57).

    Seasonality: a tilt toward Q4, and a supportive post-midterm tape

    Q4 and Q1 listings had the largest pops (+39% and +34%) and Q3 listings faded worst by month six (−33%), but quarter makes no significant difference (q ≥ 0.76). The market-level evidence is stronger because it has 76 years behind it: November and December are two of the three best S&P months since 1950, and after a midterm election the S&P rose in 18 of 19 three-month windows from 15 November.

    Macro regime: rising yields lean negative, without significance

    IPOs that listed after a 3-month rise of more than 30bp in the 10-year yield had a median day-63 return of −18% against +2% with flat yields, but the 95% CI runs from −29% to +2% and the regime test is not significant (q ≥ 0.57). High volatility (VIX above 22) bought the biggest pops (+56%). Anthropic lists with the 10-year at 5.15%, a level no IPO in the cohort saw.

    Cross-asset: no liquidity drain before large IPOs on average, a soft week after

    Across 11 large listings (Snowflake to SK hynix), the ten sessions before listing look ordinary for every asset tested: Bitcoin was up 3.6% on average (p = 0.67) and gold was flat. The Bitcoin, gold and MSTR sell-off before SpaceX, and again before Anthropic, is the exception, not the pattern. The week after listing is where something shows: S&P −1.2% (p = 0.03), small caps −1.8% (p = 0.03), high yield −0.5% (p = 0.04), VIX +2.6 points (p = 0.02), followed by a rebound over days 6 to 20 (S&P +3.1%, p = 0.05). Two cautions: none of it survives correction for 56 tests (q > 0.3), and the averages lean heavily on CoreWeave, which listed days before the April 2025 tariff crash; the medians are close to zero.

    Anthropic: a conditional plan

    What the pricing on 18 Nov tells you

    Cohort medians for the two demand groups; Anthropic's range is $70 to $78.

    Tailwinds

    • Software and AI: the most resilient sector by median (day 63 −3%), though not significantly.
    • Q4 and post-midterm: S&P up in 18 of 19 post-midterm windows from 15 Nov.
    • Valuation: about 16x to 17x run-rate revenue against about 39x forward sales for SpaceX.
    • Allocation: for anyone allocated at the offer, flipping on day 1 has been the one robust edge (+37% mean, 79% hit rate).

    Headwinds

    • Hot-deal reversal: pricing above $78 puts it in the group that fell 20% by day 63.
    • Mega size: every mega-cap in the sample reverted within 21 days (n = 3).
    • Rates: 10Y at 5.15%, +46bp in three months.
    • Supply calendar: SpaceX's 8 Dec unlock lands on Anthropic's day 13; Anthropic's own early-release windows start from day 1.
    Base · SpaceX-lite
    ~50%

    Pop of +15% to +35%, peak on days 2 to 5, down 10% to 20% from the day-1 close by day 21, near the offer by day 63.

    Bull · scarcity holds
    ~20%

    Strong days 1 to 5 that persist; well above the offer through month three. Needs yields to stop rising.

    Bear · hot deal reverts
    ~30%

    Prices above range, pops, then fades below the offer within about three weeks; −20% to −30% by day 63.

    Scenario odds are my judgment informed by the statistics above, not a model output. Anthropic is a single trade: even the best historical setup here has a per-trade Sharpe around 0.4, which means a wide range of outcomes on any one name. Not investment advice.

    Data and method

    Table view: all 43 IPOs

    Universe. 43 notable US IPOs, Sep 2020 to Jun 2026; hand-picked, so it leans to large, well-known deals (selection bias), and has no 2022 listings. Direct listings and the SK hynix ADR are excluded from IPO-stock statistics; SK hynix is included as a cross-asset event.

    Deal terms (offer, shares, proceeds, first and final ranges, upsizing) from SEC 424B4 prospectuses and pricing press releases. Float = proceeds / market cap at offer (shares outstanding × offer where disclosed). Oversubscription multiples are rarely published and are not used.

    Returns. Yahoo Finance daily and 60-minute bars. Day 1 = first close. Hourly analysis keeps only IPOs whose hourly feed includes the debut session (eToro and SK hynix were dropped).

    Statistics. 10,000-draw bootstrap percentile intervals; t-test and Wilcoxon signed-rank for single strategies; Kruskal-Wallis across buckets; Spearman rank correlation; permutation tests (10,000 random-date draws) for cross-asset windows. Benjamini-Hochberg q-values across each family of tests (28 bucket tests, 11 correlations, 56 cross-asset windows). Returns are fat-tailed and IPOs cluster in hot windows, so even the reported p-values are optimistic.

    Sharpe. Per-trade = mean / standard deviation. Annualised-equivalent = per-trade × √(252 / holding days), an upper bound that assumes back-to-back repetition. Realistic = per-trade × √(setups per year): 7.5 IPOs or 1.8 large listings.

    Anthropic facts (range, 825M shares, dates, lock-up) come from secondary summaries of the S-1/A; verify against the filing. Macro: FRED and Yahoo. Q3 IPO class: Renaissance Capital. Not investment advice.
