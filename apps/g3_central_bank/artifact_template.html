Part I · §1.3

Macro read — gauges, regime cell, curve segment

Three variables, each read spot and forward, scored −2…+2 and signed so that hot always means hawkish.

zi = (valuei − referencei) / scalei  ·  gauge = clamp( round( mean z ), −2, +2 )Reference = target or neutral where one exists, else the 10-year mean; scale = 10-year std; inverted components (unemployment, claims, NFCI) are negated. Composite = 0.4 inflation + 0.3 growth + 0.3 labour.

Distribution tree → fair value → size

Macro scenarios → probability distribution → meeting outcomes → futures prices. E[F] = Σ pⱼ Fⱼ · EV = E[payoff] − market · f* = argmax Σ pⱼ ln(1 + f rⱼ).

Per contract — expected price, market, EV

Positive EV = the internal distribution expects the contract to trade higher (lower rate) than the market. Market-tree E[F] should match the close (bootstrap consistency).

Structures on one EV scale

P&L in bp of the contract rate per unit; Kelly = fraction of the loss budget to commit (loss-normalised returns); half-Kelly is the desk size before Part-III limits; "∞" = no losing scenario (premium too cheap to be real).

Part III

Risk — shocks, strike map, expiry map, liquidity, aggregate

What can hurt the book today, and what creates a large P&L if the thesis is right?

Book MANUAL

qty in lots (+ long); options: strike in rate (%), premium in bp paid (+) per structure; strangle wings at ±25 bp around the strike.

Shock grid — P&L by shock family ($k, intrinsic re-evaluation)

Options are re-evaluated at intrinsic value under the shock (no vega / theta): understates long-option P&L and overstates short-option losses under small shocks — conservative for a long-gamma book.

Strike map — options book by contract × strike

Expiry map — what each option carries into expiry

Key question: what events occur between today and expiry, and what happens to the position if they change the market distribution? Expiry = contract last trading date; the embedded calendar covers 120 days of releases plus all meetings.
